# System Audit — July 2026

Open-ended audit of the TBR system: prediction quality, data consistency, schema, site,
and workflow. Every finding cites a specific entry, field, line, or commit. Proposals are
implementation-ready task specs. Findings are ordered highest-leverage first; a
"leave these alone" section closes it out.

Sources read in full: `CONSTITUTION.md`, `ratings.md`, `tbr.md`, `index.html`,
`README.md`, `RUNBOOK.md`, `development.md`, `api/submit.js`,
`.github/scripts/enrich.mjs`, `.github/workflows/enrich-submission.yml`, and git history.

## 1. The calibration loop leaks data — predicted→actual is recorded for only 2 of 7 queue graduates

**Broken.**

- tbr.md's "Already Read / Removed from Queue" records the prediction delta for exactly two
  books: Left Hand of Darkness ("Underperformed its 8.5 prediction") and Piranesi ("Was the
  top 9/10 prediction — a meaningful miss"). The other graduates — 1% lifesteal, The Girl
  with All the Gifts, Starter Villain, A Wizard's Guide to Defensive Baking, Royal Gambit —
  carry no prediction at all. The system's stated purpose is ranking by predicted likelihood
  of 9/10; five of seven closed loops threw away the grade.
- Mechanically, the leak is in the pipeline: `enrich.mjs`'s rating-reconcile path relies on
  Claude's *optional* ≤15-word `note` field (enrich.mjs:275) to carry the comparison. The
  deterministic code (`removeFromTBR`) has the removed entry — including its
  `**Predicted rating:**` line — in hand and discards it.
- CONSTITUTION.md's "Known risks" absorbed the Piranesi and Defensive Baking lessons but
  never the LHOD one (slow/thematic literary fiction underperforms) — even though tbr.md's
  Goblin Emperor caveat already cites that exact lesson. The constitution is the literal
  enrichment prompt (enrich.mjs:89 reads it into every Claude call), so an unabsorbed lesson
  means the automated predictor never learns it.

**Proposed change:**
- `enrich.mjs` — in `reconcileRating`, before removal, extract the `**Predicted rating:**`
  value from the matched entry block and write `Predicted X → actual Y.` deterministically
  into the Already Read line. Keep Claude's `note` as optional color, not the carrier.
- `tbr.md` — normalize existing Already Read lines to
  `- Title — Author. Read (Format). Predicted X → actual Y. [note]`, backfilling from git
  history where recoverable, else `no prediction recorded`.
- `CONSTITUTION.md` — add the LHOD lesson to Known risks; add a maintenance rule: when a
  queue book is rated, record predicted→actual, and update Known risks on any miss ≥ 1.5.

**Effort:** M. **Acceptance:** every Already Read line shows the delta or an explicit
"no prediction recorded"; the next rating-reconcile commit includes the delta without
depending on Claude's optional note; CONSTITUTION.md Known risks mentions slow/thematic
literary underperformance.

## 3. Tier placement contradicts the entries' own text and predictions

**Broken (one direct contradiction) + judgment (the rest).**

- The Goblin Emperor sits under `## Tier 3 — Worth Trying` while its own caveat says "Treat
  this as a lower-confidence Tier 2 pick, not a wait-and-see" (tbr.md:172). Text and
  placement disagree.
- The Spy Who Came in from the Cold is predicted 8.5 — tied with Baru Cormorant (Tier 2) and
  above every other Tier 2 book — yet sits in Tier 3. Sixteen Ways and The Yiddish
  Policemen's Union (both predicted 8) also sit in Tier 3 next to Dark Matter (6.5). Tier 3
  spans 6.5–8.5. Tiers encoding confidence-of-9 rather than expected score can be coherent,
  but nothing documents why an 8.5-expected book is low-confidence, and CONSTITUTION.md
  defines tiers only as "confidence of hitting 9/10."

**Proposed change:** `tbr.md` — move Goblin Emperor to Tier 2 or rewrite its caveat; decide
Spy's placement (its own "Why it's here" argues Tier 2). `CONSTITUTION.md` — one added
sentence under the tbr.md rules: tier = confidence of 9/10, predicted rating = expected
score, and they may diverge (high-expected/low-confidence books stay Tier 3).

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** no entry's caveat names a different tier than its section;
within each tier the predicted ratings overlap sensibly or the divergence rule explains them.

## 4. Tier 1 repeats the documented over-prediction pattern — and cites a book that isn't in the data

**Fixed** (2026-07-08, commit `496e545`) — checked with the owner rather than guessing: The Road's prediction dropped to 7.5/10, its "Why it's here" now argues against the profile explicitly (no dark wit, well-worn worldbuilding) and names the Piranesi/LHOD pattern instead of leaning on reputation, and it moved from Tier 1 to Tier 3 to match the lowered confidence. The "Dust" comp is now grounded: ratings.md's Wool (Silo Series) entry records the owner's actual account of Dust's ending (time-shift structure undermined the earlier eras' stakes), so the name tbr.md cites resolves to real data.

**Prediction-quality judgment (flagged as such) + one factual inconsistency.**

- The Road is predicted 9/10, but its "Why it's here" cites zero profile criteria — "the
  definitive post-apocalyptic novel," "directly relevant to what you're thinking about in the
  genre right now." That's reputation plus topical interest, which is exactly the Piranesi
  failure mode (predicted 9, got 6.5). The only closed-loop literary/thematic reads both
  missed high (Piranesi −2.5, LHOD −1.5). The constitution's 9–10 band requires dark wit and
  original worldbuilding; bleak literary fiction has neither in that register. No Country
  (8.5) is the same category, less extreme.
- Factual: both Tier 1 rationales lean on "Dust" ("earns its ending in a way Dust couldn't,"
  "Dust's ending bothered you") — but Dust appears nowhere in ratings.md. The Wool entry
  (8/10, "Silo Series") never mentions Dust's ending. The predictor is citing a signal the
  data layer doesn't contain.

**Proposed change:** `tbr.md` — rewrite The Road's "Why it's here" against the profile
explicitly and re-predict honestly (7.5–8 is what the closed-loop evidence supports; if the
9 stays, the entry should say why this book evades the LHOD/Piranesi pattern). `ratings.md` —
expand the Wool entry (or split out Dust) to record what the ending problem actually was, so
the comp exists on the record.

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** every book name referenced in a tbr.md rationale exists in
ratings.md; The Road's prediction has a profile-based justification.

## 6. The site never displays the predicted rating

**Fixed** (2026-07-08, commit `dbb4464`) — `parseBook` now extracts a `predicted` field and `renderTBRBook` shows it as a muted pill (`~9/10` style) next to the byline, reusing `.owned-pill`. reusing the `.owned-pill` styling pattern.

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** load tefleming.com; every entry with the field shows the

## 7. The Rate button is dead code on the live site

**Fixed** (2026-07-08, commit `dbb4464`) — dropped the `purchaseFormat` gate; the Rate button now renders whenever `b.purchased && !ratedSet.has(rateKey)`. Verified in a sandboxed render of Catch-22 (`purchaseFormat: null`, `purchased: true`) — the button now appears.

`renderTBRBook` (pre-fix) only showed the Rate button when `b.purchaseFormat` was set
(index.html:849–853), and `purchaseFormat` was only set when the Status/Kindle/Audible line
contained kindle/audible/whispersync. Catch-22's `**Status:** Purchased` and all 16 Scalzi
entries' `**Status:** Owned (Humble Bundle EPUB, June 2026) — purchased` set
`purchased=true` but left `purchaseFormat=null` — so no owned book got a Rate
button, and the whole rate-modal + ratedSet apparatus (index.html:501–558, 1116–1186) was
unreachable with the then-current data.

**Proposed change:** `index.html` — drop the `purchaseFormat` gate; show Rate whenever
`b.purchased` and the title|author isn't in `ratedSet`.

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** Catch-22 and the Scalzi entries show a Rate button; clicking
it opens the prefilled modal.

## 8. ratings.md data hygiene

**Fixed** (2026-07-08) — author name corrected to "Ursula K. Le Guin" and title cased to "The Left Hand of Darkness"; LHOD note expanded to a Piranesi-style post-mortem (predicted 8.5 → actual 7, profile-referenced); Soul Fraud's author (Andrew Givler) added; section retitled "7-8.5" to include the existing 8.5s. `index.html`'s score-range regex now accepts en-dash ranges as well as hyphens (verified against existing hyphenated and a synthetic en-dash entry — both parse correctly, no regressions).

**Not prevented going forward.** This was a one-time data cleanup, not a pipeline fix — nothing stops the same misspelling/casing problem from recurring on the next submission. `api/submit.js` writes `title`/`author` from the form straight into the `### Title — Author` heading verbatim for both `tbr.md` stubs (`submitTBR`) and `ratings.md` entries (`submitRating`); `enrich.mjs`'s `enrichTBR` reuses that same raw string when writing the enriched queue entry, since Claude's enrichment response schema only returns `tier`/`predicted_rating`/`why`/`caveat`, never a corrected title/author (enrich.mjs:105, :174). The only place Claude's fuzzy matching touches casing at all is the rating-reconcile step, which tolerates "punctuation/casing" variants (enrich.mjs:272) so it can *find* the right queue entry — it doesn't correct or rewrite the stored string. This is the same gap finding 2 already flagged as an unimplemented optional add-on ("have the enrichment prompt return canonical title/author casing"); that add-on is the actual fix for recurrence, and it remains open.

**Broken (small, concrete).**

- "Left hand of darkness — Ursula leguin" (ratings.md:40): misspelled author on the public
  site, and — worse — the system's biggest documented miss carries a six-word note ("Slow
  pacing - themes didn't land"), violating the constitution's own rule that notes explain
  *why* relative to the profile. Piranesi's post-mortem is the model; LHOD deserves the same
  2–3 sentences.
- Soul Fraud (line 124) has no author — schema violation; renders with a blank author line.
- "## 7-8" contains two 8.5s (The Martian, Expeditionary Force).
- Latent parser risk: the ratings score regex accepts hyphen ranges only
  (`[\d.]+(?:-[\d.]+)?`, index.html:761). tbr.md ranges use en-dashes ("7.5–8"); if an
  en-dash range ever lands in ratings.md the book silently vanishes from the Ratings tab.

**Proposed change:** `ratings.md` — fix "Ursula K. Le Guin", expand the LHOD note with the
miss post-mortem, add Soul Fraud's author, retitle the section "7–8.5" (or move the 8.5s).
`index.html` — add `–` to the score-range character class as cheap insurance.

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** site renders the correct author, no blank-author entry, and an
en-dash-range test entry still parses.

## 9. The Scalzi bundle turned the queue into six pseudo-tiers, half of them sequels

**Taste-call — I'd change it; the owner may not.**

The `## Tier — Scalzi Bundle · ...` headings are crafted to trick the parser's
`startsWith('Tier')` check (the provenance note at tbr.md:214 admits this). Result: one
author is 16 of ~30 queue cards; the ranked-by-confidence ordering breaks on the page
(Scalzi's 7.5–8 picks render *below* Tier 3's Dark Matter at 6.5); and 11 of the 16 cards
are sequels whose entire content is "read book 1 first" — they're consequences of reading
book 1, not queue decisions, and add scroll without information. development.md's schema
("Tier 1/2/3/Already Read") is now false.

**Proposed change:** `tbr.md` — keep one card per series entry point / standalone
(The Collapsing Empire, Lock In, Old Man's War, The Android's Dream, Redshirts, Fuzzy
Nation, Kaiju Preservation Society, Agent to the Stars), fold each into the real Tier 2/3
by predicted rating with its Owned status; list sequels as a one-line "also owned:
books 2–N (…)" inside the entry-point card; move the provenance note to the non-rendered
notes section. Update development.md's schema note.

**Effort:** M. **Acceptance:** site shows exactly Tier 1/2/3 + Already Read; every owned
Scalzi book is either a card or a line inside its series card; no predicted-7.5 book renders
below a predicted-6.5 book.

## 10. Range predictions hedge the calibration the system exists to do

**Taste-call.**

"6.5–7.5" (The Magicians, Red Rising), "7–7.5" (nine Scalzi entries), "7.0–7.5"
(Discount Dan). A 1-point range on a 0.5-increment scale makes misses mushy — the LHOD
lesson only exists because "8.5" was falsifiable. The rating scale itself forbids ranges;
predictions should match. Confidence already has a home: the tier.

**Proposed change:** `tbr.md` — collapse each range to its point estimate; `CONSTITUTION.md`
and the tbr.md checklist — state "predicted rating is a single value in 0.5 increments";
`enrich.mjs` — change the predicted_rating field rule in the prompt from
`"7", "7.5", or "8-9"` to a single value.

**Effort:** S. **Acceptance:** `grep -E 'Predicted rating.*[–-]' tbr.md` returns nothing.

## Leave these alone — they're working

- **api/submit.js** — input validation (score increments, slug guard), sha-based writes,
  restrictive CORS, the 401 → "PAT likely expired" surfacing. Solid.
- **enrich.mjs architecture** — Claude does fuzzy matching, deterministic code does the file
  surgery, and "Claude claimed a match but the heading didn't resolve → throw, don't guess"
  is exactly the right failure posture. Failure comments on the PR likewise.
- **RUNBOOK.md** — genuinely good ops doc: the dual-PAT-storage warning and the "Action ✗
  with no comment = the PAT itself is dead" diagnostic are the kind of thing people usually
  learn the hard way.
- **The rating notes on the 9–10 and most 7–8 books** — comparative and profile-referenced
  (the Checquy "access structure" insight, the HWFWM restraint analysis, the Piranesi
  post-mortem). This is the system's core asset.
- **Site rendering quality** — consistent `e()` escaping (no XSS holes found), the
  nested-paren format-note and scoreless-DNF parser fixes, collapse/keyboard handling.
- **The core schema** — three tiers + why/caveat, with honest caveats as a first-class field.
